Dark patterns detected

severity 2 asymmetric choice step 0
“The 'Accept' button is styled as a primary blue button while 'Reject' is a secondary button, making the business-preferred option (accepting cookies) more visually prominent than the user's likely desired action (rejecting).”
The cookie consent banner presents 'Accept' as the primary action with a more prominent blue button, while 'Reject' is a secondary button. This visual hierarchy makes it easier for users to accidentally accept cookies rather than reject them, which could obstruct the user's goal of canceling a subscription by adding an unnecessary step that is harder to complete correctly.
severity 2 false urgency step 6
“This offer expires in 00:52”
The countdown timer creates artificial urgency to pressure the user into accepting the retention offer before the cancellation can be completed.
severity 2 misdirection step 3
“The 'Continue' button is styled as a prominent red button while 'Never mind, take me back' is a plain text link, drawing attention to the business-preferred action of continuing the cancellation process rather than the user's goal of canceling.”
The visual hierarchy makes the 'Continue' button appear as the primary action, which could mislead users into thinking they must continue the process rather than being able to easily reverse their decision.
severity 2 obstruction step 3
“The page requires users to answer two questions ('To proceed, please confirm your decision' and 'What is the main reason you are leaving?') before they can proceed with cancellation, adding unnecessary steps to the cancellation process.”
Requiring users to complete a survey before allowing them to cancel their subscription adds friction and delays the cancellation process, which is a common obstruction pattern in subscription cancellation flows.
severity 2 trick question step 6
“Keep my profile and stay on Netflix Basic (free) — I understand my Premium plan will end”
The checkbox text is confusing because it implies the user is 'staying' on a free plan, which could mislead users into thinking they're not fully canceling their subscription when they actually are.
severity 1 confirmshaming step 6
“Hold on — we have something for you”
The 'Hold on' phrasing implies the user is making a mistake by trying to cancel, creating a subtle guilt-trip to make them reconsider their decision.
