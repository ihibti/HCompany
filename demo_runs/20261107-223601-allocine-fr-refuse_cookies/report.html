Dark patterns detected

severity 2 asymmetric choice step 0
“The 'Accept' button is visually more prominent than the 'Reject' option, which is presented as a less prominent text link. The 'Accept' button is styled as a primary action, while 'Reject' is a secondary option that requires additional effort to locate and select.”
The 'Accept' button is designed to be the primary action, making it more visually prominent than the 'Reject' option. This creates an asymmetric choice where the user's desired action (refusing cookies) is visually weaker and less accessible, potentially leading to accidental acceptance of non-essential cookies.
severity 2 forced action step 1
“The only way to refuse non-essential cookies is to pay 2€ for a one-month subscription ('Accéder au site pour 2€ TTC pendant 1 mois sans cookie publicitaire'). There is no free option to refuse cookies.”
The user is forced to either pay for a subscription or accept cookies to continue using the site, which is a form of forced action that hinders the goal of refusing non-essential cookies.
severity 2 misdirection step 1
“The 'j'accepte' button is styled in bright yellow and is more visually prominent than the 'Je m'abonne' button, which is grey and less noticeable. This draws attention to the option that accepts cookies rather than the option to refuse them.”
The design uses color and visual hierarchy to make the 'accept' option more prominent, steering users toward accepting cookies rather than refusing them.

ePrivacy Directive Art. 5(3)EU
Consent required for non-essential cookies
Directive 2002/58/EC requires prior consent before storing or reading non-essential trackers on a user's device (transposed in France by Art. 82 of the Loi Informatique et Libertés).
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2002/58/oj
CNIL Délibérations 2020-091 / 2020-092FR
Refusing cookies must be as easy as accepting them
The CNIL guidelines and recommendation on cookies require that refusing be as simple as accepting. In January 2022 the CNIL fined Google €150M and Facebook €60M because refusing took more clicks than accepting.
https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cookies-et-autres-traceurs/regles/cookies/lignes-directrices-modificatives-et-recommandation
GDPR Art. 7(3)EU
Withdrawing consent must be as easy as giving it
Regulation (EU) 2016/679 requires that it be as easy to withdraw consent as to give it.
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj
DSA Art. 25EU
Ban on deceptive interface design by online platforms
Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 forbids online platforms from designing interfaces that deceive or manipulate users, including giving more prominence to certain choices, repeatedly requesting a choice already made, and making termination harder than subscribing.
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065/oj
Directive 2005/29/ECEU
Unfair Commercial Practices Directive
Prohibits misleading and aggressive commercial practices, including non-contractual barriers to terminating a contract.
